Identity, limits, records, and recovery
Control and evidence

A saved response alone does not prove a finding.

ControlProposed implementationAcceptance condition
Identity and approvalNamed operators. Approver roles. Short-lived service identities. Scope bound to exact assets, actions, and expiry.An unauthorized identity cannot approve or execute a campaign.
SecretsDedicated service accounts. Protected credential files initially. A credential service when workflows require it.Test VMs receive only synthetic, limited credentials. Rotation revokes previous access.
Execution limitsTyped tool actions. Request and time budgets. Cross-process accounting. A stop control.The runner stops at the limit. Stop prevents further actions and preserves partial records.
Network isolationUse the physical plan VLANs and default-deny firewall rules. Disable host forwarding.Agent and target guests cannot reach ordinary LAN devices or management services.
Evidence protectionAuthenticated submission. Separate reader and writer identities. Hash checkpoints copied to protected storage.A runner can submit records. It cannot read, replace, or delete retained records.
MonitoringCollect service logs, denied traffic, runner state, model load, storage capacity, and time synchronization status.A reviewer can correlate each request with its campaign and target event.
Reset and backupKnown VM images. Synthetic state seeds. Separate backups of configuration and evidence.A restore produces expected state. Record retention and deletion follow a defined policy.
Supply chainVersion-controlled configuration. Pinned dependencies and image digests. Recorded model hashes. Quarantine downloads.Fresh hosts reproduce approved versions. Update checks precede promotion to runner images.
Data handlingSynthetic data initially. Classify inputs and raw records. Review redaction before sharing.Records and prompts contain no unapproved secrets. External model access requires separate approval.
